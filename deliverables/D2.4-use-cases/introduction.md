# Introduction

The Montandon Global Crisis Data Bank brings together, in a single open and standards-based catalogue,
the hazard, impact and response data that humanitarian actors and Earth Observation services produce
around a disaster. Under the current activity, Development Seed and MapAction are connecting
ESA-managed Earth Observation products — from the International Charter "Space and Major Disasters" and
the Copernicus Emergency Management Service — into that catalogue, so they can be discovered and
analysed alongside the humanitarian record maintained by the IFRC.

This document was **not part of the originally planned deliverables**. It has been prepared **at the
request of ESA**, to build a shared and more concrete understanding of the **use cases** that the
analytical work will target in the **next phase** of the activity. Its purpose is to align ESA, IFRC,
MapAction and Development Seed on what the tools will actually do — the questions they answer, the data
they draw on, and the users they serve — before detailed development begins. The use cases are
therefore **proposed**: the examples, figures and rankings shown here are illustrative, and the final
scope will be confirmed as the analytics and tools are developed.

We describe three use cases that build on one another, moving from **risk** to **impact** to **action**:

- **Use Case 1 — Population and Infrastructure Risk Exposure**: how many people and which assets are
  exposed to a given hazard, before any event, as a baseline for preparedness and planning.
- **Use Case 2 — Post-disaster Impact Estimation**: who and what falls within the observed extent of an
  actual disaster, using Copernicus remote-sensing products, in the hours and days after it strikes.
- **Use Case 3 — Operational Response Prioritisation**: which affected areas to prioritise for response
  in the first 24-72 hours, by combining the exposure and impact analyses with Montandon's disaster
  history and operational data — and feeding that prioritisation back to the International Charter.

Each use case is presented with the same structure: its **context and relevance** (the decision it
supports and why it matters), the **data sources** it uses — with a deliberate, heavy reliance on
Montandon data — and the **methodology** by which the analysis is produced. Together they illustrate
how the integration of ESA Earth Observation with Montandon crisis data can be turned into practical,
reproducible analytics for humanitarian decision-making.
